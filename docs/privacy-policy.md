# Privacy Policy

_Last updated: October 16, 2026_

Squared Away is run by {{OPERATOR}}, {{LOCATION}}. It helps small trade businesses answer their
email: it sorts incoming business email, writes reply drafts in the owner's own voice, and holds
every draft until the owner approves it. This policy explains what we collect, why, where it goes,
and how to make us delete it.

The short version:

- We read your Gmail only to sort your business email and write your reply drafts.
- Email text is sent to one outside service, Anthropic (the company behind the Claude AI), only to
  do that work. It is not used to train AI models.
- We delete email body text after 30 days.
- Nothing is sent from your Gmail unless you approve it in the app (for example by tapping **Send
  reply**), or you have switched on autopilot for a kind of email yourself.
- You can disconnect Gmail or delete your account at any time from Settings.

## Who this policy covers

This policy covers the owners and staff who sign in to Squared Away ("you"), and people who join
our waitlist or ask for a free Squared Away Growth call on our website. It also explains how we handle
information about the people who email your business (your customers and suppliers), because their
messages pass through Squared Away.

Squared Away is a tool for businesses. It is not meant for children, and we don't knowingly
collect information from anyone under 16.

## What we collect

**When you join the waitlist:** your name, email address, trade, team size, and anything you choose
to add (phone number, a note), and where you came from: the campaign tags in the link you
followed (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) and the name of the website that sent you,
not the page (we keep the first one we saw, so we know which links bring people to the waitlist).
We email you once to confirm you're on the list, and send ourselves a notice that someone joined
(it says only that a sign-up came in, with no name, number or email address). To stop spam, we briefly keep a scrambled (hashed) version of your
IP address to limit how many times a form can be sent; it can't be turned back into your IP
address and is deleted within three days.

**When you ask for a free Growth call:** your name, phone number, email address, trade, the services
you want help with (for example Google ads or reviews), when you asked, and anything you add in the
note, and where you came from, kept the same way as for the waitlist. We use it only to contact
you about Squared Away Growth. If you're already on the app
waitlist, we add your request to that entry and keep everything you gave us before. Asking for a
call doesn't sign you up for the app or let you sign in. The same scrambled IP address limit applies to
this form. If you followed another Squared Away owner's referral link while we offer referrals,
we keep that link's code with your request, so that owner can be thanked once if you become a
Growth client.

**When you sign in with Google:** your name and email address. Sign-in asks Google only for "who you are" (`openid`, `email`, `profile`). It does not give us
access to your email.

**When you agree to our terms:** the date and time you ticked "I agree to the Terms of Service and
the Privacy Policy" (when you sign in, or when we ask again after the terms change) and which
version of the terms you agreed to, so we can show that you agreed.

**When you connect Gmail:** this is a separate step, after a screen that explains what Squared Away
will and won't do. We ask Google for two Gmail permissions (named as Google's screen names them):

**What we store from Gmail:**

- Who each message is from and to (and the address replies should go to — the "Reply-To" a
  website form sets, so a reply reaches the customer and not the form), the subject, and the
  date. When you connect, we read the last
  30 days of mail; after that, new mail is added as it arrives.
- For conversations in your inbox: the text of the messages, so we can sort them and write replies.
  We don't download the text of mail outside your inbox (for example archived newsletters).
- A one-line summary of what each conversation is about (for example "Wants a quote for a deck
  repair"), a one-line reason when something needs you or a draft is written a certain way (for
  example "Lawyer's letter about a lien"), the category we sorted it into, and facts pulled from it: the kind of job, a street

  building-permit details (permit number, who issued it, inspection time and result, and any
  corrections the inspector asked for).
- The reply drafts we write, so you can review them in the app, and the notes our checks put on
  them (for example "Mentions $450 — that price isn't in the email or your profile").
- **How you write:** a description of your writing (your usual greeting, sign-off, length and
  common phrases) and up to five short example replies in your style. These examples are written
  by the AI, which is told to use placeholders instead of customers' names and details; before we
  save the description and the examples, our own code also replaces the email addresses, phone
  numbers, prices and street addresses it recognizes with placeholders. To
  learn it we read up to 200 of your sent emails from the last year: when you first connect,
  again about once a week (skipped if you have edited your style yourself), when you tap
  **Re-learn from my sent mail**, and again automatically if a read stops part-way. Those sent
  emails are used only for that and are never stored.
- How many photos or files an email had (never the files or their names).
- Your Gmail "send mail as" addresses (like office@ or info@ on your account), so a reply goes
  out from the address your customer wrote to. Read with the Gmail access you already gave; we
  don't read their settings or signatures.
- Prices you quoted (the amount, the kind of job and the date), kept as your price memory. Only
  you see them, and they are never sent to the AI.
- Which invoices are marked paid (or "not owed to me"), a marker on conversations that were ever
  a complaint (so we never offer to ask that customer for a review), which customers were
  sent a review request, and when you marked a conversation as answered by phone (**I called
  them**).
- Which kind you moved an email to, when you change it yourself (**Wrong kind? Move it**).
- If you tell us, why a quote was lost (price, timing or went with someone else).
- Which lead website or phone service a conversation came through (for example Thumbtack or
  Google Voice), which of our "needs you" checks it matched (as short labels like "emergency" or
  "outside your towns", not text from the email), whether you marked it done, and when you marked
  a complaint fixed or closed its check-in.
- For a first reply to someone you've never written to, which kind of first reply the draft is
  (a short label like "urgent" or "asked about price", not text from the email), and when we let
  your phone know about an emergency email.
- Whether an email looked like a gas smell, a carbon monoxide alarm, an electrical fire sign,
  sulfur-smelling water or an emergency (and when), why an email was marked as trying to instruct
  the assistant (a short code, not the text), and whether you said it was a normal email.

**What you tell us:** your business details (business name, trade, services, service area, hours,
price list, payment terms, policies, things you never promise, words you never use, short
seasonal notes, your review link, the towns you work in, and the license and registration
numbers you enter), your settings, and, if you turn on text
alerts, your own mobile number. Also the sender rules you set (an email address or a company's web
address, and what to do with its email), the people you mark as VIPs, short private notes you write

you remove them or delete your account.

**Phone notifications:** if you turn them on, we store the address your browser's push service
gives us for that phone (the push service is run by Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft, depending
on your browser). The notification we send through it is empty; your phone then asks us for the
counts to show, so no counts, names or email content pass through the push service. If your
phone's notification service keeps refusing our notifications for 7 days, we forget that phone's
notification address.

**Text alerts:** for each text alert we send you, whether your carrier delivered it (the message's
id from our texting provider, its status and error code — never the text or your number), kept 30
days. If texts stop because you replied STOP or the number can't take texts, we note which.

**Payments:** card details are handled by Stripe and never reach us. We keep your Stripe customer
and subscription IDs and your plan, and, when your plan covers fewer Gmail addresses than you've
connected, which of them is paused (nothing in a paused one is read or deleted).

**What happens in the app:** a record of actions (for example "draft created", "reply sent",
"Gmail connected"), including for each reply you approved when you approved it and whether from a
phone or a computer; daily counts of AI use and estimated cost; and, for replies you edited
before sending, counts of what you changed (how many words, whether you changed the greeting or
sign-off) and the change in tone, which we use to suggest updates to your writing style; and
which trial emails we sent you and whether you stopped them. These records hold IDs, counts and
yes/no marks, not email text.

**Cookies:** we use only the cookies needed to keep you signed in, to protect the Gmail connection
step from forgery, and to remember for a few minutes that you ticked "I agree" while Google signs
you in, plus small cookies that remember this phone's display choices
(left-handed layout, high contrast, offline approvals). We don't use advertising or tracking
cookies, and we don't run analytics that follow you. We count visits to our public pages (the
home page, the Growth page, this policy and the terms) with Vercel Web Analytics. It sets no
cookies and doesn't follow you from site to site or from one day to the next; it sees the page,
the campaign tags in the link, the site you came from, your rough location (country, region and
city, worked out from your IP address, which it doesn't keep), and your device type, operating
system and browser. It never runs on the signed-in app. If you switch on offline approvals, your
phone keeps a copy of your queue (in the browser's storage) and the approvals you make without
signal until they reach us; signing out deletes them. Private customer notes are left out of
that copy.

## How we use it

- To sort your business email and put what needs you first.
- To write reply drafts and follow-up drafts (at most two per conversation, ever) for you to
  approve, and notes you can send: asking for the deposit after a customer accepts your quote,
  asking for a review when you tap **Ask for a review**, or, once a quote has had both follow-ups
  and a week of quiet, asking whether the customer went another way, only when you tap it.
- To write a warm first reply when someone you've never written to asks about a job: it thanks
  them and asks only what's missing, or asks for the best number and the address when something
  is going wrong right now. It's a draft like any other and waits for your **Send reply**.
- To reword a draft when you ask, by tapping a quick change or telling it what to change. If you
  speak the change, your browser turns your words into text using its own speech service (run by
  Google, Apple or Microsoft, depending on your browser); we receive only the text.
- To keep your quotes, money owed and customers in order, and to remember the prices you quoted.
- To show **Call** and **Text** buttons, we look for a phone number in the customer's email when
  you open your queue. We don't store the number. Calls and texts go from your own phone:
  Squared Away never calls or texts your customers.
- To point out a request from outside the towns you work in, and to tell you when you've worked
  for a customer before.
- To send a reply when you approve it, and, during your trial, a practice copy to your own address
  when you tap **Send it to yourself instead**.
- To send you your morning summary, a monthly summary at the start of each month, an email if
  Google stops letting us read your Gmail (plus one reminder two days later), during your trial a
  short email on day 2 and on the last day and one a week after it ends (counts only, each with a
  link to stop them), and, if you turn them on, text alerts and phone notifications. The morning summary lists the kinds of email waiting and how long they have waited, with links into the app, and this month's money so far as totals (quotes you won, invoices paid and what's still owed); the monthly summary has counts, your most common kinds of job and where requests came from; the Gmail alert names your own Gmail address; texts and notifications have counts only. None of them contain a customer's name or words.
- If you leave **Emergencies, even at night** on, an email that sounds like an emergency (a leak,
  no heat, no power, a gas smell) reaches your phone right away by the texts and notifications
  you turned on, even between 9pm and 7am. That includes a voicemail or text message your phone
  service emails you (such as Google Voice), even though it comes from a "no-reply" address. It
  says only "An emergency email just came in." — once per conversation, at most 3 a night and 6 a
  day — and it goes only to your own phone, never to the customer or caller. You can switch it off
  in Settings.
- To run your account: billing, support, keeping the service working and secure.

We don't sell your information or your customers' information. We don't use it for advertising.

## Sending and autopilot

Every reply waits in your queue until you approve it, by tapping **Send reply** (or **Send N
replies** after reviewing several together). You then have 20 seconds to undo it before it goes.
We don't draft replies to lead-website notices or voicemail emails — answer those on the
website or from your own phone — and we never draft or send to an email-to-text address,
because that would text your customer.
During your three-day trial, Squared Away won't send anything to a customer: its own sending is
switched off in the code. You can still send yourself a practice copy of a draft: it goes only to
your own Gmail address, as a new email marked "[Practice]", never to the customer. Your drafts are
in your Gmail Drafts folder too, so you can send one from Gmail yourself if you want.

Autopilot is off unless you turn it on, one kind of email at a time, and only on plans that
include it after your setup call. It only becomes available for a kind of email after you have
sent ten of those replies exactly as drafted. It never answers complaints, bills with an amount on
them, anything flagged for you, or someone you haven't written to before, and it never sends a
reply that mentions a price or an amount. Follow-ups, deposit and review notes, and replies to a
customer accepting a quote always wait for you. Every autopilot reply waits in your queue for 10
minutes with a **Hold it** button before it is sent, and every check runs again just before
sending.

## Squared Away Growth

Squared Away Growth is a separate, one-on-one service: we help trade businesses with ads, social
media posts, their Google Business Profile, reviews and websites. Growth never uses Squared Away's
access to anyone's Gmail. Nothing from your email, or your customers' email, is used for Growth
work, ads or posts. Gmail data we receive through Google's APIs is never used for Growth (see
Limited Use below). If you hire us for Growth, what we need from you (such as photos or access to
your ad accounts) is agreed with you directly, outside this app.

**If you're a Growth client:** you can sign in to a Growth dashboard. For it we keep which Growth
services you have, the day Growth started, whether it is active, paused or ended, and the time
zone your months are counted in; the updates {{OPERATOR}} posts for you (a title and text in
{{OPERATOR}}'s own words) and when we emailed you about each one; and the numbers
{{OPERATOR}} enters after each monthly check-in with you (new leads, calls, jobs won, money from
jobs won, reviews gained, ad spend, and a short note). The dashboard shows only those numbers,
as you told them to {{OPERATOR}}. It never reads anything from the inbox app: no email, no
counts or totals from your inbox, quotes or drafts. If we invite you to Growth before you have
an account, your invite notes the services and start date until you first sign in.

## Where your information goes

We use these service providers. Each gets only what it needs:

- **Google** — your Gmail, through the permissions above.
- **Anthropic** — the text of the emails being sorted or answered, with each one's sender (name
  and email address) and subject, your business details and your writing-style description, sent for the task at hand only: sorting a message, learning your
  style from your sent mail, writing a draft, follow-up, deposit or review note, rewording a draft
  when you ask, or comparing a reply before and after you edited it to see how the tone changed.
  Your price memory is never sent. Neither are your private customer notes or your sender
  rules. New email from a sender you set to Always junk usually isn't sent to the AI, unless it
  looks like a complaint, a legal matter or a building department, or you moved that
  conversation yourself. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, this data is not used to
  train their models. Anthropic may keep it for a limited time under its own policies, for example
  to prevent misuse.
- **Vercel** — hosts the website and app, on servers in the United States (its US East region),
  and counts visits to our public pages (Web Analytics, as described under Cookies).
- **Neon** — hosts our database (in the United States).
- **Inngest** — runs our background jobs. It sees IDs and counts, not email text.
- **Stripe** — takes payments. It receives your email address and your business name (or your
  name, if no business name is set), and keeps its own payment and customer records. Its checkout
  page may also ask for your billing address, which Stripe uses to work out sales tax; Squared Away
  doesn't store it. Stripe saves that address, and the name you enter there, on its customer record
  of you.
- **Resend** — delivers our emails to you (your invite with the sign-in link, your Growth
  dashboard invite, a "New update from Squared Away Growth" email with the update's title and a
  link, a one-time "your first drafts are ready" email, during your trial a short email on day 2
  and on the last day and one a week after it ends unless you stop them, the morning summary, the
  monthly summary and the Gmail-access alert and reminder), which never contain a customer's name
  or words; the email confirming you
  joined the waitlist; and four kinds of notice to us: when someone joins the waitlist or asks for
  a Growth call (each says only that a sign-up or request came in, with no name, number or email
  address), when someone pays (it names only the plan and whether the setup fee was included),
  and when an account's AI cost runs high for its plan (the business name, or the owner's email
  address if no business name is set, and the account's AI cost, plan price and usage counts).
- **Twilio** — only if you turn on text alerts: delivers texts to your own phone (the code that
  confirms your number, then counts and a link).
- **Your browser's push service** (Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft) — only if you turn on
  phone notifications: delivers an empty ping to your phone.
- **Sentry** — only if switched on: receives reports of errors only, with your account ID, email
  addresses masked, and no email content.

We may also share information if the law requires it, or to protect the safety of people or the
service.

**Where it's processed:** Squared Away runs in the United States. The app runs on Vercel's servers
in the United States and our database is hosted in the United States; the providers above process
what they receive under their own policies.

## How long we keep it

- **Email text** (message bodies, Gmail's preview text, draft text and the check notes on drafts)
  is deleted after 30 days by a daily job. The street address, dates and inspector's corrections
  pulled from a conversation are deleted once its newest email is more than 30 days old. Mail
  older than 30 days is never stored with its text in the first place.
- **Who, when (including a form's Reply-To address), subject, summaries, the one-line reasons and categories**, the kind of job, amounts mentioned, invoice
  details (number, amount, due date, paid or not) and permit details (permit number, who issued
  it, inspection time and result) are kept so your inbox, quotes, money owed and activity history
  keep working, until you disconnect that Gmail address or delete your account.
- **Your price memory, quote won/lost marks, why a quote was lost, invoice paid marks, complaint
  markers, review-request records, lead-source, done and fixed marks, first-reply labels, when
  we alerted you to an emergency, and the counts and tone change of edited replies** stay after the
  30-day email-text purge, until you disconnect that Gmail address or delete your account.
- **Your writing style, style suggestions, business details, settings, activity history and AI
  usage counts** are kept until you delete your account.
- **Private customer notes** stay until you remove them, disconnect the Gmail address they were
  written under, or delete your account. **Sender rules and VIPs** stay until you remove them or
  delete your account.
- **When you agreed to the terms, and which version,** is kept until you delete your account.
- **Your mobile number for text alerts** is kept until you remove it in Settings or delete your
  account. **Whether each text was delivered** (the message's id, status and error code) is kept
  30 days.
- **Phone notification addresses** are deleted when you turn notifications off, when that phone
  stops accepting them (7 days of refusals), or when you delete your account.
- **Waitlist entries** are kept until you ask us to remove them. When we invite you, we note the
  date on your entry.
- **Growth client records** (your services, start date, status, the updates posted for you and
  the monthly numbers entered for you) are kept until you delete your account, or ask us to
  remove them. Ending Growth closes the dashboard but keeps them.
- **Growth call requests** are kept until you ask us to remove them (call, text or email us). If
  you send another request from the same email address, the newest one replaces the one before;
  the name and trade from your first entry stay on it.
- If you get a refund or open a card dispute, we keep its amount and dates (no card details)
  after you delete your account, like Stripe's own payment records.
- If your trial ends or a payment fails, we stop reading your mailbox and stop using the AI, but
  we don't delete your data for that reason alone.

## Your choices

- **Disconnect Gmail** (Settings): removes our access at Google straight away and deletes
  everything we stored from that Gmail address — messages, conversations, drafts, the counts of
  what you changed in those drafts, and the prices remembered from them. Your writing style and
  any style suggestions waiting for you, your business details, your activity history and your AI
  usage counts stay until you delete your account.
- **Delete my account** (Settings): deletes your account and everything in it from Squared Away,
  after one confirmation. Stripe keeps its own payment records. We keep only the amount and dates
  of any refund or card dispute (no card details, no link to you). First it cancels your
  subscription straight away (not at the end of the paid period), and any other plan Stripe still
  has for you, and removes our access at Google. It also
  removes your invite, so coming back needs a new one. Stripe also keeps its customer record of
  you (your email address and your business name, or your name if no business name is set; if you
  paid while sales tax was on, also the billing address you entered on Stripe's checkout page, and
  the name you entered there in place of the one we sent).
  Counters we use to limit how often things can be done hold only an account ID and a count; they
  are deleted within three days. Drafts already in your Gmail Drafts folder stay in your Gmail. Our database provider keeps short-term backups for
  recovery, so deleted data can remain in those backups for a short time before it is gone for
  good. This doesn't remove a waitlist entry or Growth call request you sent from our website; ask
  us and we'll delete it.
- You can also remove our access any time at
  [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions).
- To see, correct or delete what we hold about you — including if you are a customer of one of
  our users — contact us below.

## Security

Connections are encrypted (HTTPS). Google access tokens are encrypted before they are stored and
are never shown in the browser or written to logs. Our logs never contain email text, access
tokens or customers' personal details. Only the account owner can see their mail in the app; our
admin page shows account health and counts, plus the waitlist and Growth call requests people send
us from the website, and for Growth clients what {{OPERATOR}} entered for them.

If a security breach exposes your information, we'll tell you as soon as we can and without
unreasonable delay, as Massachusetts law (Chapter 93H) requires, and we'll notify the authorities
that law names.
